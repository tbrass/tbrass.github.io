    Sources, scope & classroom notes

    This atlas follows settlement by newcomers, alongside the much longer history of Native peoples. Dakota and Ojibwe homelands overlap, extend beyond Minnesota, and do not disappear after treaties. Other Native nations and Métis communities also shaped this region. The year slider shows broad patterns; events within a year are summarized together.

    The modern state outline is a location guide before 1858. The 1849–1857 western extension is a schematic cue, not a surveyed territorial boundary. Natural regions, treaty focus areas, routes, arrows, and town density are simplified teaching graphics. Selected 1805, 1837, and 1851 agreements are shown; this is not a complete treaty or reservation map. Treaty signing, ratification, surveys, and settlement happened at different times. There was no single “1862 treaty”: war, imprisonment, and 1863 federal removal laws are shown separately. Artifact panels are modern illustrations, not archival reproductions.
